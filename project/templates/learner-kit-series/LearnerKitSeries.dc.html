Learner Trail Kit series

A plan for the kits we sell to people who work in Salesforce — or want to. The hard question here is not what to build. It is what a paid kit offers that the free official training does not.

The thesis
The free official training teaches you to click the buttons. It cannot tell you whether you should, what good looks like afterward, or how to get twelve colleagues to actually use the thing you just switched on. That gap is the product.
The accidental admin
Handed the org because they were good with computers. Nobody trained them, and they have been guessing for two years.
They are not job hunting. They want to stop feeling like a fraud in their own system.
The working admin
Certified and newly hired. The credential opened the door; now they have to do the job, often as the only Salesforce person there.
They buy a kit for the reps they have not had yet, and buy again if the first respected their time.
The career changer
Studying toward a first Salesforce role, with a dev org and no one to ask whether their work is any good.
All three are program prospects, entering by different doors — this one at Foundations, the other two at Guided or Mastery.
Two of the three buyers already have the job. Write for them first. Page 1

Every learner kit answers six questions
1 · What is it, in language you could use with your manager.
2 · Why would you turn it on, and when you should not.
3 · How do you enable it, step by step, with proof at each stage.
4 · What does good look like — the practices that separate a competent setup from a working one.
5 · How do people adopt it, including what to say and who to tell first.
6 · What to watch once it is live and other people depend on it.
Question 5 is the one nobody else answers. Do not let it get cut for space. Page 2

Learner kit seriesOrder and production

What to build, in what order

The order, and why
Reports first. It scores 29, every seat needs it, and a buyer sees the result in an afternoon.
Then duplicates and importing, finishing the first series. A bundle needs a complete series; an unfinished one is three kits on a shelf.
Permission sets opens the second. Highest-judgment topic on the roster, and the real test of whether the format survives a hard subject.
Pricing
Single kit at the same list price as the nonprofit line — one price across both audiences is easier to defend than a split anyone can see through.
Bundle by series, not by count — on sale only when the series is complete.
Every kit purchase credits toward tuition on any program they later join. Print it on the placemat in every learner kit — it turns a small test purchase into a deposit.

Done means all six — not five
· Every step performed in a clean dev org by someone who did not write the kit.
· Each step names what you should see, not just what to click.
· Troubleshooting lists failures we hit, not ones we imagined.
· Adoption names who to tell first, and gives them the words.
· Video chapters match the numbered steps exactly.
· Trail OS holds the record; the PDF was generated from it.
Effort and the dry run
Two days writing, one filming, half a day of fixes. Then hand it to someone new and watch them work without helping — where they stop is the real weak point, and it is never where you expected.
A kit a month is the cadence, and the month a topic is thin is the month a bad kit ships. Skipping costs less than a refund.
Revisit whenever a kit ships or a score changes. Page 4
Learner kit seriesFour series

Four series, each with one outcome

Named for what the buyer can do afterward, not for the features inside. A series is what turns twelve unrelated kits into something worth subscribing to.

Answers, not exports
3 kits · start here
Reports and dashboards · Finding and merging duplicates · Importing a spreadsheet
The promise
You can answer a question with the system instead of exporting to a spreadsheet and hoping.
Why it leads
It contains our highest-scoring kit and the fastest visible payoff. Buyers judge the line on this.
The right people, the right access
3 kits · second
Permission sets and profiles · Why can this user not see this record · Setting up a new user
The promise
You can set someone up correctly, and explain to their manager why they can see what they can see.
Why it matters
Ties for highest judgment on the roster, and carries the highest stakes — access mistakes get found out the hard way.
Changes people accept
3 kits · L07 already shipped
Activity Capture · Validation rules · Salesforce on a phone
The promise
You can roll out a change that affects other people without it quietly failing three weeks later.
Why it is ours
All three score 5 on adoption. Nobody else sells this, because it cannot be taught as a click path.
Make the system fit the work
3 kits · later
Your first Flow · Record types and page layouts · List views people actually use
The promise
You stop working around the system and start shaping it to how your team actually works.
Why it waits
Flow is the most-taught topic anywhere. Enter it last, with three series of format behind us.
Twelve kits across four series. At one a month, that is the year. Page 5

Entry depends on what you hold
Real project work for a real organization, reviewed and signed by someone who will vouch for it.
Foundations, Guided, or Mastery — which one depends on what you already hold and what you need next.
Kit spend comes off any of them.
Tuition credit — decided
What someone spends on kits comes off tuition on any program they apply to. Any, because buyers arrive at different points — some are studying, some already hold the certification, some already have the job.
Say it on the placemat in plain terms: every kit you buy comes off your tuition when you join a program. No asterisk — the moment it needs a footnote it stops working.
It also answers the objection we will hear most: should I buy kits or save for the program? Now the honest answer is both.
Mechanics to settle
A cap? Uncapped is cleaner to say, and hard to exploit at a single-kit price.
An expiry? Twelve months keeps it honest without feeling grudging.
Trail OS should hold this on the contact record, so nobody has to find receipts.
Where Explorer's Trail sits
Individual learners belong on Explorer's Trail, not Compass — that is settled in the program design.
Open: does the kit library come with Explorer's Trail the way it does with every Compass tier? If it does, the credit and the membership start competing.
