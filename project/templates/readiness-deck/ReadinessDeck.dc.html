Readiness vocabulary

Four words for saying where something actually stands — and why we use them exactly.

Transition Trails Trail Crew orientation

Why the words exist

"Live" requires evidence, not a label.

The problem

Everyone means something different by "done". One person means the code exists, another means a partner is using it. Both are telling the truth and neither status can be trusted.

What precision buys

A status you can read without re-checking it. That is the entire return — and it disappears the moment one person uses the words loosely.

The four words

Implemented

The code, authorization, persistence and workflow behavior all exist and work.

Pilot or gated

The workflow exists but is limited by scope, release, or validation gates.

Configuration-dependent

Behavior needs external credentials, scopes, metadata, or a healthy connector.

Planned

The production contract or the operating policy is not complete yet.

Trail OS today

The same platform, described honestly. Nothing here is a criticism — it is what the words are for.

The change chain
Implemented — Case through Release, with authorization and audit behind every transition.
Build With Me
Pilot or gated — configured against one pilot Case, reading a single Salesforce hierarchy.
Mobile app
Pilot or gated — built. Device, role, offline and handoff validation all remain.
Slack, Drive, Salesforce
Configuration-dependent — each can break on scopes or credentials rather than code.
Agentforce routing
Planned — sessions work as a POC. When Penny hands off is still a decision.

Readiness in design artifacts

A mock that implies something ships is the same lie as a status that does.

In UI kits and decks

Label a screen that shows a gated workflow. A prototype is read as a promise by anyone who was not in the room when it was made.

The four status fills

Green, amber, teal and slate — all clear 3:1 on Trail Light, so they hold at indicator size. Never use a divider color as a status fill.

Before you publish a status

Name the evidence, then pick the word.

If you cannot point at what proves it, the honest word is one step back from the one you wanted to use.
