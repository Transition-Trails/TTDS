Where should you actually do this?

A free developer org is the right answer most of the time — but not always. Some licensed features are absent from Developer Edition entirely, and if this kit covers one of those, no amount of clicking will find it. Choose before you waste an afternoon.

Developer org
Free, permanent, yours
Use it when
You are learning, and the feature exists on Developer Edition. This is the default.
Not when
The feature is licensed separately, or you need more than two users to test it.
Never holds real data.
Sandbox
A copy of your real org
Use it when
You have a real org and want to try the change against your own setup first. This is the right answer for most nonprofits.
Not when
You do not have one yet — ask your admin, or check whether your edition includes one.
Config copies over; data may not.
Production
Your live org, real people
Use it when
You have already tried it somewhere else, told the people affected, and know how to reverse it.
Not when
You are still learning what the setting does. "I'll just try it and see" belongs anywhere but here.
Mistakes here affect real people.
Decide in three questions
1
Is this kit's feature available on Developer Edition?
Check this kit's access notes first. If the answer is no, a developer org cannot help you and the choice is sandbox or production.
2
Do you have a real org you are responsible for?
If yes, use a sandbox — it mirrors your real configuration, which a clean developer org never will. If no, the developer org is your answer.
3
If it has to be production, can you reverse it?
Read the rollback page first, and tell the people affected. Production is sometimes the only option — unannounced never is.
The honest catch
Several features our kits cover are not on Developer Edition. If you follow the dev-org sheet and then cannot find the feature, you did nothing wrong — you need a sandbox instead.
Write down which org you worked in. Six months on, "I set this up" is far less useful than "in the sandbox, 14 March."
