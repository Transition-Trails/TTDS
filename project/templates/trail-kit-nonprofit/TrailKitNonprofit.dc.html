Trail Kit 01 · Nonprofit editionTab 1 · Why

Your relationships live in people's inboxes.

Every email to a major donor, every exchange with a partner agency, every thread about a family you serve — most of it sits in one person's mail, invisible to everyone else. Activity Capture puts that history on the record it belongs to, automatically. Here is what that changes.

0
hours a week your staff spend logging emails by hand
1 day
to set up, using this kit, with no consultant
$0
extra license cost on most Salesforce editions
1
Right now
When someone leaves, the relationship leaves with them. Their inbox goes dark and nobody knows what was promised.
After this
The correspondence is on the donor or contact record. Whoever picks it up can read the history before they call.
2
Right now
Your board asks how many times you contacted lapsed donors last quarter. You genuinely cannot say.
After this
You can answer from a report instead of from memory, because the activity is captured whether or not anyone remembered.
3
Right now
Two staff contact the same funder in the same month, neither knowing the other did.
After this
The last touch is visible on the record, so the second call either does not happen or happens on purpose.
4
Right now
Your grant report needs evidence of engagement, and you reconstruct it from memory and sent folders the week it is due.
After this
The engagement history is already there, dated, on the records it belongs to. You pull it instead of rebuilding it.
If you need to make this case to someone else, the three rows above are the argument — in their language, not Salesforce's. The cost of doing nothing is the part people forget to mention.

Trail Kit 01 · Nonprofit editionTab 2 · Decide

Four decisions, before any clicking.

These are judgement calls about your organization, not technical settings. Write your answers down — Tab 3 asks for them.

1 · Whose email gets captured?
Start with the two or three people who talk to donors and partners most. Not the whole staff, not on day one.
2 · Email only, or calendar too?
Email first. Calendars often hold personal appointments, and that conversation deserves its own week.
3 · What must never be captured?
If you serve clients whose privacy is protected, name those boundaries now and in writing. This is the decision that matters most.
4 · Who signs off?
Your ED, and your board if you hold protected client data. Tell staff what will be visible before it is.
Nothing in your org to test with? Three CSV files came with your purchase. Five addresses in them are yours to replace before importing — the README explains why nothing can arrive pre-matched.
These four questions are about your organization — nobody knows it better than you. The technical half comes later, and it is the easy half. Captured email becomes visible to more colleagues than the sender expects. Say so in plain words, in writing, before you switch it on.

When it doesn't work

Almost every problem here is one of five things, and none of them mean you broke something. The first two account for most of it. Change one thing at a time, then wait.

Nothing at all appears, hours after activating
Why it happens
The email account is not fully authorized. Your email administrator may need to grant consent at the organization level, which is a separate approval from anything you did in Salesforce.
What to do
Open the configuration and check the connected account reads Connected. If it does not, send your email administrator the consent request and wait — do not change the sync settings while you wait.
It works for you but not for a colleague
Why it happens
Capture is per-person. Being inside the configuration is not enough — each person also has to connect their own mailbox once.
What to do
Confirm they are assigned to the configuration, then have them complete the connection prompt themselves. Watch them do it; this step is usually skipped rather than failed.
Emails appear, but on the wrong record or none at all
Why it happens
Matching happens on the email address. If a donor writes from a personal address that is not on their contact record, there is nothing to match to.
What to do
Check the address the email actually came from against the addresses on the record. Add it as a secondary email, and expect to keep doing this — it is data hygiene, not a fault.
Meetings are not showing up
Why it happens
Calendar sync is off. If you followed Tab 2, this is deliberate and correct — you chose email first.
What to do
Nothing to fix. When you are ready for calendars, revisit decision 2 and have the privacy conversation before you switch it on.
It worked for weeks, then quietly stopped
Why it happens
The connection expired — usually after a password change, a security policy update, or a mailbox migration.
What to do
Reconnect the account in the configuration. Add a recurring reminder to check this quarterly; the sync fails silently, so nobody reports it.
Before you ask anyone for help, write these down
What you expected to see · what you saw instead · which user it happened to · the exact time you activated · whether the connected account says Connected. Five lines gets you a useful answer; "it is not working" gets you a week of questions.

How to turn this off

Nobody tells you this part, and it is the part that makes trying something safe. If the pilot does not suit you, if a colleague raises a privacy concern, or if the timing turns out to be wrong — here is the clean way back.

1
Tell people first
Same list you told when you switched it on. Turning it off quietly is how trust in the system gets lost.
2
Deactivate the configuration
Open the configuration and set it to inactive. This stops new capture immediately; you do not need to delete anything.
3
Leave the connection alone for now
Disconnecting the mailbox as well is a bigger step and rarely necessary. Deactivating is enough to stop the flow.
4
Write down why
One line on the notes page. If someone revisits this in six months, the reason matters more than the setting.
What stays
Activity already captured stays visible on your records. Turning the feature off stops new capture — it is not an undo of what has already synced.
What does not come back
If you delete the configuration rather than deactivating it, its settings are gone and you rebuild from scratch. Deactivate first. Delete only when you are sure.
What to send when you switch it off
We have turned off the email capture we switched on earlier this month. Anything already logged stays where it is — nothing has been deleted. New email will stop appearing on records from today. We are pausing it because [reason], and we will look at it again [when]. Come to me with any questions.
Four sentences: what changed, what did not, why, and who to ask. Silence is what makes people distrust the system — not the change itself.
Deciding to switch something off is not a failure. Trying it, learning it does not fit your organization, and reversing it cleanly is a good outcome.

If you want someone on staff properly trained

You do not need a certification to use this kit — you just used it. But if your organization keeps hitting the limits of what nobody has been taught, these are the two credentials worth funding. Both are Salesforce’s own, and both are recognized outside your organization, which matters to the person earning them.

Salesforce Platform Foundations
The entry credential
What it proves
That you understand the platform’s core concepts — how objects, records and relationships fit together, and what Salesforce is actually for.
Who it is for
Anyone starting out, including staff who inherited an org and have never been taught how it works.
Salesforce Platform Administrator
The working credential
What it proves
That you can configure and maintain an org: users and permissions, data model changes, automation, reports and dashboards.
Who it is for
The person who actually administers a Salesforce org, or is about to become that person.
Which one first
Foundations first, then Administrator. Foundations takes weeks, not months, and it is what makes the Administrator material make sense rather than feel like memorization. Skipping it is the single most common reason people stall on Administrator and conclude they are not cut out for this.
Salesforce publishes free study paths for both, and the exams can be retaken. If cost is the blocker, ask us on the Digital Compass — there are nonprofit discount programs most teams do not know exist.
Why fund this
A trained staff member costs less than the consultant you call when nobody knows how the org works. It is also one of the few professional development budgets people stay for.
Worth knowing
These same two credentials are what our learner programs require, so a certified staff member could also mentor or host — ask us on the Digital Compass.
