Foundations Trail · Sprint 2 · story revision

Make the platform limit the lesson

Three new columns for the traceability matrix, and a revision of the five intake stories so a Developer Edition constraint becomes curriculum instead of a surprise. Written against the matrix as it stands in Week 9 of Cohort 0 testing.

01 · The model change

Three columns, and a convention for splitting

Your matrix already carries Build Notes and a Pass/Fail Test. What it cannot currently say is where a criterion is provable and what the platform refuses to do. Both matter the moment a learner works in a client sandbox instead of their own dev org.

Environment
Where this criterion is proved: learner dev org, TT sandbox, or client sandbox. "Passes in a dev org" and "passes in a client sandbox" are different claims — and only one of them counts as client work.
Platform constraint
What the edition or the environment will not do. Named up front, so nobody burns a week discovering it — and so the coach knows a failure here is not a knowledge gap.
Workaround
The approach that gets the criterion proved anyway. This is the column employers care about — it is the difference between someone who followed a guide and someone who has shipped.
S2-03 became .a and .b because a story outgrew its week in real testing. That should be the documented convention rather than a one-off: a story may split into lettered children, each carrying its own acceptance criteria and Definition of Done, and the Week Map allows a story to span weeks. In Trail OS that is a parent/child relationship on the story record — so a coach can split mid-cohort without editing a spreadsheet, and the three sheets cannot drift apart again.
02 · The five intake stories

Revised, with what the platform refuses to do

S2-01 · rewrite Multi-Channel Email-to-Case Week 5 · Service & Support ~11%
Platform constraint
Nobody owns mailboxes at hch.org, and Email-to-Case routing addresses require inbox access to verify. The story assumes three real addresses that cannot exist.
Workaround — test before rewriting
Gmail plus-addressing. name+info@gmail.com, +help, +grants all deliver to one inbox and each verifies independently as a distinct routing address. Three channels, one mailbox, no domain needed — the multi-channel build stays intact.
AC-3 threading — retarget, do not delete
"Client replies to a notification; reply attaches to Case #1001 with no duplicate" is the one criterion that cannot be faked, because it needs a real outbound email and a real reply. Retarget it: the learner sends the reply from a second personal address and verifies the Email Message record attaches to the original case. Fiddly, real, and provable.
S2-02 · decide ownership Automated Queue Routing Week 5 or Week 7 — currently both
No platform constraint. The problem is bookkeeping: the detail sheet marks S2-02 complete in Week 5, and the Week Map lists it active again in Week 7. A learner cannot answer "what am I building this week" when two weeks claim the same story.
Recommendation: Week 7 owns S2-02. Week 5 owns record types, queues, and Email-to-Case — the containers. Week 7 owns the routing logic that fills them, paired with S2-04, and the exam concept it teaches (one active assignment rule per object, entries evaluate top-down, first match wins) belongs with the Sales & Marketing domain in Week 7.
S2-03.a / .b · keep the split Web-to-Case form, then the public site Week 6 → Week 6 and 7
The split was the right call — the LWR digital experience site, guest user access, and guest API permissions are a week of work on their own, and .a is provable without them. Formalize it: .a ships the form and its hidden record type; .b ships the public site the form lives on. Two Definitions of Done, two demos.
Platform constraint on .b
Guest user access and guest API access must both be enabled, and guest permissions are deliberately restrictive. This is where learners get stuck, and it is not a Web-to-Case problem.
Workaround
Verify in an incognito window as the acceptance criterion already says. Add a named check that the guest user profile has Create on Case — the single most common cause of a silent form failure.
S2-04 · annotate Auto-Response & Routing Notifications Week 7 · Service & Support ~11%
Platform constraint
Developer Edition caps daily outbound email to external addresses. Auto-responses count against it, and a learner testing three channels repeatedly will hit the ceiling and see silent failures that look like broken configuration.
Workaround
Name the limit in the story and cap the test plan: one submission per channel, verified, then stop. Teach the learner where to find the org's email log so they can tell a limit from a misconfiguration — which is itself an exam-adjacent skill.
S2-05 · the constraint encounter Loop Protection Flow Week 8 · Process Automation ~16%
This is the story where your instinct pays off. A learner cannot make a real out-of-office autoresponder fire at them — but they do not need to. Sending an email whose subject begins "Automatic reply:" proves every acceptance criterion, because the flow keys on the subject, not on the sender's intent. All three tests stay provable as written.
The added deliverable
Email-to-Case is out-of-the-box, and out-of-the-box still let ghost records flood a queue — which is why this story exists at all. Make the learner write that down: what the standard feature does not handle, what you built to cover it, and what it would have cost Elena if you had not. One paragraph, in her language.
This is Explore → Select from the RESOLVE handbook, and it is the most credible entry a Decision Log can contain. Nobody invents having hit a platform limit.
